# Ajay's writing style — observed

A running record of what Ajay changes when he edits a draft, so future drafts
need fewer edits. **Read this before drafting a post.**

Every rule here is derived from an actual before/after, not from taste. Each
carries the evidence and a confidence level. Rules with one observation behind
them are marked as such — treat them as leads, not laws.

**Evidence base as of Sept 11, 2026:** one full draft-vs-edit comparison (Labor
Day 2026 Midterm Prediction), plus the published text of two earlier posts whose
drafts are no longer available. That is thin, and it grows one post at a time —
see *Keeping this current*.

---

## Titles

**Concrete and descriptive beats clever and abstract.** `Three Ways to Guess an
Election` → `Labor Day 2026 Midterm Prediction`. The published title says what
the post is and when it is. (1 observation, but consistent with
`Fiscal Responsibility in America` and `State of the Iran War`.)

## Openings

**Open on the occasion, not on a rhetorical hook.** Cut:

> Every couple of years someone asks me who's going to win, and I've started
> answering with a question: which method do you want me to use?

Replaced with a plain statement of why he's writing now — Labor Day, midterms
two months out, time for a snapshot. He wants the reader oriented, not warmed
up. (1 observation. Note the fiscal post opens on a concrete fact — USAID's
budget — rather than a hook, which points the same way.)

## Section headings

**Plain noun labels.** He rewrote every one of mine:

| mine | his |
|---|---|
| What's actually on the table | The State of Congress before the Race |
| Method one: add up the polls | Method one: Polling |
| Method two: add up the money | Method two: Markets |
| Method three: the keys | Method three: The Keys |

He kept `Ohio and Texas` and `So what do I actually think` unchanged — so the
rule isn't formality, it's that a heading should name its subject rather than
perform a verb. (Strong: 4 changes in one post.)

## What he cuts

**Aphorisms about the analysis itself.** All three of these went:

> That's the tell for any forecast you read this fall. **The headline number is
> the easy part. The width is where the honesty is.**

> ...because this is where most forecasts quietly cheat.

> Anyone selling you a keys model as precision is selling you numerology with a
> table around it.

**This is not a dislike of figurative language.** His own published prose is
full of it — "USAID was a streaming subscription," "amputating the leg to save
the shoe," "government theater performed in units they know the audience can't
size-check." The distinction: **metaphor that explains the subject stays;
epigram that comments on the craft of analysis goes.** (Strong: 3 cuts in one
post, and the contrast with his own published voice is unambiguous.)

**Swipes at other practitioners.** Two of the three cuts above are also digs at
other forecasters. He'll criticize politicians and institutions freely; he
doesn't posture against peers doing the same work he's doing.

**Methodological self-flagellation.** He cut my entire paragraph reporting the
keys model's R² of 0.42, its 16-seat RMSE, and the seven-predictors-on-twenty-
observations problem.

He did **not** cut uncertainty as such — he kept the correlated-polling-error
passage, kept "nowhere close to a safe bet — call it 55-60%," and kept the
whole section showing the keys model returning an impossible answer. The line
seems to be: **caveats that make the analysis smarter stay; caveats that make
the author sound unsure of his own work go.** Statistical diagnostics read as
the second kind even when they're the first.

If a limitation genuinely matters, put it in plain language and tie it to a
consequence, rather than reporting fit statistics.

## What he adds

**Connective tissue to his ongoing projects.** He added "which I plan to update
as the race continues to unfold" to the tracker link, and "I have it as a
longshot on the tracker" to the South Carolina passage. He wants posts to read
as part of a continuing body of work, not standalone.

**Practical caveats in his own register:**

> The weakness is that betting markets swing wildly from day to day, and this
> method will probably have a lot of variance in the following weeks.

**Explicit ownership of the call.** He added "So here are my official
predictions roughly 2 months out:" before the conclusions.

**Casual disclosure of AI help.** He wrote "(with the help of some AI
extrapolation from Lichtman's work, thanks Claude)" himself. Don't add this
unprompted, but don't be coy about it either.

## Voice and mechanics

- **"Let's" for walkthroughs.** "Let's plot every race" (his) over "Plot every
  race" (mine); "Let's look at them" in the fiscal post.
- **Conversational asides**, often parenthetical and unpunctuated-run-on: "(and
  what it actually found was a list of programs it politically disliked by the
  way, 0 waste fraud or abuse)".
- **Colon where I reach for an em-dash.** "The market says 51%: a coin flip."
- **Bolded thesis sentences** standing as their own paragraph, for the one claim
  a section exists to make.
- **"blogpost"** as one word.
- **He flattens my dramatic contrasts.** "That's a genuinely strong signal, and
  it's also the least interesting thing in this post" → "However, there's one
  point of disagreement that's worth analyzing for a moment."

## Process notes

**He does not proofread closely.** His edit introduced "the expected are still
51," "rigged some districts on way or the other," and a missing sentence-final
period, and left them for me to catch. **Flag typos explicitly rather than
assuming they're intentional voice** — but never silently "fix" phrasing that
might be deliberate.

**He edits voice, not substance.** In this post he changed essentially no
numbers, no structure, and no analytical claims. The draft's job is to get the
analysis and the architecture right; he'll handle how it sounds. Don't
pre-emptively flatten the prose to guess at his voice — get the argument right
and let him tune it.

## Keeping this current

**Update this file at the end of the session that publishes a post, before the
draft leaves context.** That is the only moment both versions exist — the draft
as written and the note as Ajay edited it. He does not want duplicate copies of
posts kept in the vault, so nothing is archived for later comparison and the
differences are genuinely lost once the session ends. One post at a time, while
it is fresh, is the whole mechanism.

When updating:

- **Raise a rule's confidence when a second post confirms it.** Most rules here
  rest on a single observation and say so.
- **Delete a rule the moment a later post contradicts it.** A stale rule is
  worse than a missing one, because it will actively steer the next draft wrong.
- **Label anything new as single-observation** until it recurs.

Do not add rules inferred from his published prose alone. Published text shows
what he writes; this file is for what he *changes about a draft*. Those are
different things, and only the second one makes the next draft better.
